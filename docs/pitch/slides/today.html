Be precise about what is proven. In a one-off local test against a production EDC and registry (a local instance, not a production deployment), the app synced a selection of 200 vehicles (303 twins with their children) from a real Digital Twin Registry; the data plane wanted an EDR, so every submodel was read through the company's own EDC — one negotiation per shared asset, then plain reads — and nothing failed. In the Tractus-X umbrella lab: three companies, each with its own EDC, registry and app, read their direct partners and pass a quality alert Tier-2 → Tier-1 → OEM with the acceptance coming back. In the same local test, the app read its direct suppliers' parts through their own production connectors: 356 supplier parts are cached, from three suppliers. Of the six suppliers synced so far, two delivered every linked twin, three answered but publish none of the linked twins yet — a data question to settle with them, not a connectivity one — and one refused the negotiation. The links figure is 1,988 to 8 suppliers: an earlier count also included links between the company's own parts. The 7.5 minutes were measured before partners and twins were read in parallel and tokens reused until they expire; it has not been measured again. Not yet proven: notifications with a real partner in production — that is the pilot.
